**AI Workforce Map's unique value proposition:** The only tool that assesses individual employees for specific AI-adjacent roles (Operator, Approver, Workflow Translator, QA/Risk Reviewer, Change Champion) using scenario-based assessments that measure behavioral judgment in realistic AI workflow situations.
**No existing competitor** combines:
1. AI-specific content (not generic personality/cognitive)

- SHL (broadest capability + SJT expertise)

## 2. AI-Specific Assessment Tools
### 2.1 TestGorilla — AI Skills Tests
TestGorilla offers an "Artificial Intelligence" test evaluating core AI concepts including machine learning, NLP, predictive analytics, game theory, propositional logic, and robotics. It targets data scientists, AI engineers, and developers — not business users in AI-adjacent roles.
TestGorilla has positioned itself for "AI fluent talent" hiring, with a library of 350+ skills tests. However, these test technical AI knowledge, not behavioral judgment in AI-assisted workflows.
**Gap vs AI Workforce Map:** Tests technical AI knowledge (ML algorithms, NLP, coding) — completely different from assessing whether someone can approve AI outputs, translate business workflows into AI-compatible processes, or champion AI adoption.
### 2.2 Vervoe — AI-Powered Job Simulations
Vervoe offers AI-graded skill assessments with 300+ templates and job simulations. It uses AI to grade responses and rank candidates. Features anti-cheating measures and an AI assessment builder.
**Gap vs AI Workforce Map:** Powerful assessment platform but content is generic. No pre-built AI-adjacent role assessments. Lacks the specific AI Workforce Map role framework (Operator, Approver, Workflow Translator, QA/Risk Reviewer, Change Champion).
### 2.3 Organizational AI Readiness Platforms
Several platforms assess organizational AI readiness (strategy, infrastructure, data, governance) but do not assess individual employee capability:
| Platform | Focus | Gap vs AI Workforce Map |
|---|---|---|
| Microsoft AI Readiness Assessment | 7 pillars: Business Strategy, AI Governance, Data Foundations, AI Strategy, Organization & Culture, Infrastructure, Model Management | Org-level assessment, not individual |
| Cisco AI Readiness Index | 6 dimensions: Strategy, Infrastructure, Data, Governance, Talent, Culture. Benchmarks against global database. | Org-level, not individual capability |
| Bain & Company / WGS AI Readiness Tool | 34-question assessment across 5 dimensions: AI Strategy, Use Case Development, Technology & Data, Talent & Capabilities, Governance & Operating Model. Free for public sector | Public sector org-level; not individual |
| Mercer AI Readiness Report/Index | Assesses across mindset, people, process, technology | Framework/research, not an assessment product |
### 2.4 Gap Analysis: The White Space
No tool in the market specifically assesses individual employees for AI-adjacent workflow roles. The landscape breaks down into:
1. **Generic talent assessment tools** (Harver, Plum, PI, Wonderlic, SHL, Arctic Shores) — measure personality, cognitive ability, motivation. Not AI-specific.
2. **Technical AI assessment tools** (TestGorilla AI test, coding assessments) — measure ML/AI engineering skills. Not for business users.
3. **Organizational AI readiness platforms** (Microsoft, Cisco, Bain, Mercer) — assess company-level maturity. Not individual employees.
4. **AI training platforms** — deliver AI literacy training but don't assess role-specific capability.
**AI Workforce Map uniquely occupies the white space** — assessing individual employees' behavioral judgment for specific AI-adjacent roles through scenario-based methodology.

## 4. DOL/Government Frameworks
### 4.1 U.S. Department of Labor AI Literacy Framework (February 2026)
The DOL released a comprehensive AI Literacy Framework on February 13, 2026 — the first federal definition of AI literacy for the American workforce.
**Definition:** "A foundational set of competencies that enable individuals to use and evaluate AI technologies responsibly, with a primary focus on generative AI, which is increasingly central to the modern workplace."
**Five Foundational Content Areas:**
| Content Area | Description | Example Competencies |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Understand AI Principles | Grasp what AI is and how it works | Pattern recognition, capabilities/modalities, training/inference, hallucinations/accuracy limits, human oversight |
| 2. Explore AI Uses | Understand real-world workplace AI applications | Productivity tools, information support, creative assistance, decision-support systems |
| 3. Direct AI Effectively | Interact with AI to produce useful results | Contextual framing, prompting techniques, supplying relevant input, iterating on outputs |
| 4. Evaluate AI Outputs | Assess quality and usefulness of AI-generated outputs | Verify factual accuracy, assess completeness/clarity, spot gaps/errors, apply human judgment |
| 5. Use AI Responsibly | Use AI ethically and safely | Protect sensitive information, follow policies, avoid misuse/harm, manage risks |
**Mapping to AI Workforce Map roles:**
- "Evaluate AI Outputs" → QA/Risk Reviewer, Approver
- "Direct AI Effectively" → Operator
- "Explore AI Uses" → Workflow Translator
- "Use AI Responsibly" → All roles
The DOL framework primarily focuses on individual tool utilization — prompting, evaluating outputs, and responsible practices. It does not yet encompass the orchestration, delegation, and oversight skills needed at higher levels. AI Workforce Map goes beyond basic literacy to assess role-specific behavioral judgment.
### 4.2 NIST AI Standards
NIST's primary focus in 2025–2026 has been on AI risk management, cybersecurity, and safety standards. Key initiatives:
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF):** Guidance for trustworthy AI development and deployment
- **AI Agent Standards Initiative (Feb 2026):** New initiative to develop industry-wide standards for AI agents across sectors
- **AI Standards Development Evaluation:** Framework for evaluating AI standards including terminology, TEVV methods, risk management, security/privacy
NIST does not currently offer workforce competency standards. However, NIST's AI RMF concepts (trustworthiness, risk management, governance) align with the QA/Risk Reviewer and Approver roles.

## 5. Assessment UX Best Practices
### 5.1 Optimal Assessment Length
- Gartner: Most candidates prefer assessments between 10–30 minutes. Too short = less credible.
- Oregon State University: Longer assessments associated with higher completion rates among quality applicants — lower-quality applicants drop out early ("healthy attrition")
- Corvirtus (35+ years of data): Up to 20–25 minutes for entry-level; up to 45 minutes for management/executive. Completion rates consistently exceed 90% for well-designed assessments.
- Once an applicant makes it past the first one-third, they are unlikely to drop out due to length.
- Cangrade: Assessments with more honest time estimates upfront had higher completion rates.
**Recommendation:** Target 20–30 minutes for the scenario-based assessment. Provide accurate time estimates upfront. Lead with engaging, realistic scenarios.
### 5.2 Scenario-Based Assessment Engagement
**Realism and Relevance:**
- Scenarios must mirror actual workplace challenges, not sanitized case studies
- "Most training scenarios fail because they're too predictable" — design for ambiguity and competing priorities
- Job-relevant assessments are preferred by candidates over abstract puzzles/games
**The 5 Cs Framework (EMERGO methodology):**
1. **Context** — the setting and background
2. **Challenge** — the problem to solve
3. **Choices** — possible pathways and solutions
4. **Consequence** — feedback from each choice
5. **Culmination** — reflection and synthesis
### 5.3 Anti-Cheating / GenAI-Proofing
- Arctic Shores positions itself as "ChatGPT-proof" because task-based assessments observe behavior, not knowledge recall
- Vervoe features anti-cheating measures: plagiarism detection, question randomization, geolocation detection
- TestGorilla's 2025 Skills-Based Hiring Report: job seekers who feel "good" about employers using AI are paradoxically more likely to cheat
**Implication:** Scenario-based assessments with branching decisions and judgment calls are inherently more GenAI-resistant than MCQ or knowledge-recall formats.
### 5.4 Assessment UX Best Practice Summary
| Element | Recommendation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 20–30 minutes | Gartner sweet spot; Corvirtus data on 90%+ completion |
| Format | Scenario-based with branching decisions | Most engaging and GenAI-resistant; measures judgment, not recall |
| Realism | Use real AI workflow scenarios from target industries | Relevance drives engagement and retention |
| Progress | Show progress bar and accurate time estimate | Honest estimates increase completion; progress bars reduce dropout |
| Feedback | Provide personalized AI role profile upon completion | Every participant gets value back |
| Access | Mobile-friendly, no login required | Mobile-first + 20–25% completion boost from no-login |
| Anti-cheat | Behavioral judgment focus; branching paths; reasoning articulation | Tasks \> questions for GenAI resistance |
| First third | Lead with most engaging scenarios | Dropout in first third determines completion |

2. **Scenario-based AI judgment:** Competitors either use trait measurement or technical tests — none use realistic AI workflow scenarios

4. **Alignment with government frameworks:** DOL's AI Literacy Framework creates a validation foundation, but no tool yet translates it into role-specific assessment
5. **Consulting framework operationalization:** BCG's 70% people insight, McKinsey's superagency concept, and Deloitte's human-AI work design all identify the need — but none offer an assessment tool
### 6.3 Potential Risks
1. **SHL could build this:** With 300+ people scientists and the broadest assessment portfolio, SHL has the R&D capability to develop AI-specific SJTs. Monitor SHL product announcements closely.
2. **Harver/Pymetrics could pivot:** Their neuroscience-based approach could be adapted for AI readiness scenarios. They already support talent development and internal mobility.
3. **Consulting firms could productize:** Deloitte, BCG, and McKinsey all have frameworks — any could build a standardized assessment tool.
